Ancient Khmer Stone Inscription Analysis

A computational heritage project for documenting, enhancing, segmenting, and reading Khmer stone inscriptions from temple and museum contexts. The goal is to support epigraphers with reproducible visual analysis tools while preserving fragile historical evidence for future study.

Abstract-style overview

Ancient Khmer inscriptions are an important source for studying language, religion, governance, and cultural exchange in mainland Southeast Asia. However, many surviving inscriptions are carved into uneven stone, partially broken, covered by patina, or photographed under uncontrolled lighting. These conditions make simple thresholding and conventional OCR pipelines unreliable.

This project investigates a practical computer vision pipeline for Khmer epigraphic material: image quality assessment, stone-texture suppression, inscription region localization, text-line and glyph candidate segmentation, and low-resource recognition. The work is designed as an assistive system rather than a replacement for expert epigraphers, emphasizing interpretable outputs, visual evidence, and human-in-the-loop correction.

Challenge

Low contrast, cracks, missing strokes, biological weathering, and relief shadows obscure the carved script.

Data curation

Images are organized with metadata for source, surface quality, inscription visibility, and annotation confidence.

Annotation

The pipeline targets inscription masks, line polygons, glyph candidates, and expert-verifiable transcription units.

Evaluation

Results will report visual quality, segmentation accuracy, recognition behavior, and epigrapher review feedback.

Methodology

Clean, reproducible analysis pipeline

Inspired by modern computer vision project pages: concise claims, visible data context, and transparent status without overstating publication acceptance.

Stage 1

Image enhancement and surface normalization

Stone inscriptions combine pigment-like intensity variation with true geometric depth. The enhancement stage studies contrast-limited preprocessing, shadow reduction, local texture filtering, and learned restoration to separate carved strokes from stone grain and lighting artifacts.

Stage 2

Layout analysis on non-paper documents

Unlike printed pages, inscriptions may follow damaged baselines, wrap around cracks, or disappear at broken edges. The layout module focuses on inscription region detection, line grouping, polygon boundaries, and confidence maps that make uncertain areas easy to inspect.

Stage 3

Low-resource Khmer script recognition

The recognition stage considers historical variation across centuries and the limited availability of labeled examples. Candidate approaches include metric learning, synthetic augmentation, self-supervised visual pretraining, and expert-in-the-loop correction for ambiguous glyphs.

Stage 4

Human-centered research outputs

The intended outputs are not only predicted text strings. The project prioritizes readable overlays, uncertainty visualization, structured metadata, and exportable annotations so historians and epigraphers can verify every decision.
