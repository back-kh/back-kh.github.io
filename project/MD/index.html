Document Understanding for Non-Latin Low-Resource Documents

This project studies how machines can read, locate, and answer questions from real-world documents written in complex scripts. The focus is on low-resource and non-Latin languages, especially Khmer, Burmese, Lao, Chinese, and Japanese, where OCR errors, sparse datasets, and layout variation still limit reliable document AI.

Research Goal

Detect document regions, reading order, tables, key-value pairs, and form fields in native-script documents.

Build multimodal VQA systems that answer layout-dependent questions from text, visual cues, and spatial relationships.

Reduce bias, data leakage, and evaluation shortcuts that can overstate performance for low-resource languages.

Paper 1: Layout Analysis

Published Research

KH-FUNSD: A Hierarchical and Fine-Grained Layout Analysis Dataset

KH-FUNSD establishes a foundation for Khmer document understanding by addressing the lack of annotated resources for business-document layout analysis. It supports fine-grained modeling of document structure, including headers, lists, tables, form fields, and key-value relationships.

Unlike many Latin-script benchmarks, Khmer documents include zero-width spacing, dense consonant clusters, and varied typography. These features make region detection, reading-order recovery, and structural parsing more difficult for models trained mostly on English or other high-resource data.

Paper 2: Multimodal Visual Question Answering (VQA)

Under Review

Two papers detailing our multimodal Visual VQA framework for low-resource languages are currently under peer review. This page summarizes the motivation, core problems, and research direction without releasing unpublished experimental details.

Why VQA is difficult for low-resource documents

Document VQA asks a system to answer questions such as “What is the invoice total?”, “Which date belongs to this field?”, or “What value is in the third row of the Lao table?”. For low-resource languages, the problem is harder because the model must combine imperfect OCR, document layout, visual cues, and language understanding at the same time.

High-resource benchmarks often hide these difficulties: they have cleaner scans, stronger OCR, more public datasets, and more pretrained language coverage. Khmer, Burmese, and Lao documents receive less research attention, so evaluation must be designed carefully to avoid inflated scores and to measure true document comprehension.

Proposed multimodal fusion direction for non-Latin document VQA.

Lacking and leaking resources

Low-resource languages have few annotated document VQA datasets, limited OCR ground truth, and inconsistent fonts. At the same time, small datasets are vulnerable to leakage, where repeated templates, copied questions, or near-duplicate pages can enter both training and testing and make results look stronger than they are.

Complex scripts and tokenization

Khmer, Burmese, and Lao use stacked marks, syllable-level composition, and spacing conventions that differ from Latin scripts. A single visual word can be split incorrectly by OCR or tokenizers, causing the model to miss the entity needed for an answer.

Layout-grounded reasoning

Many answers depend on where text appears, not only what it says. The model must align OCR tokens, bounding boxes, visual regions, table cells, checkboxes, stamps, and handwritten marks to avoid confusing similar fields across the page.

Low research attention

Non-Latin low-resource languages are often underrepresented in document AI benchmarks and pretrained models. This creates weaker baselines, fewer reusable tools, and less community feedback for practical problems faced by local institutions.

Methodology direction

Spatial-aware multimodal fusion

The framework combines visual embeddings from document images, language embeddings from multilingual encoders, and geometric features from bounding boxes so answers can be grounded in both content and location.

Leakage-resistant evaluation

The evaluation plan separates document templates, question types, and visual styles where possible. This helps test whether the system generalizes to unseen documents instead of memorizing frequent forms or repeated answer patterns.

Cross-lingual document retrieval

Beyond direct question answering, the learned multimodal representations can support search across scanned native-script PDFs, invoices, forms, and administrative records in Southeast Asian languages.

Research in progress

The Visual VQA framework and broader layout-analysis benchmarks for Khmer, Lao, and Burmese are in active development. Datasets, methodology details, and publication links will be added after peer review is finalized.
