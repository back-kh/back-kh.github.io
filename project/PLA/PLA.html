PALM-SEA: Historical Palm Leaf Manuscript Analysis for Southeast Asia Regions

Nimol Thuon*, Jun Du, Jianshu Zhang, Zhenrong Zhang, Jiefeng Ma, Ranysakol Thuon, Panhapin Theang, Chan Theara Sok

NERC-SLIP, USTC, China iFlyTek Research, China Institute of Technology of Cambodia Universitas Gadjah Mada, Indonesia

* Corresponding author

Project Overview

The PALM-SEA project is dedicated to the digital preservation and analysis of historical palm leaf manuscripts from Southeast Asia. These manuscripts are invaluable cultural artifacts that document religious, literary, and scientific knowledge spanning centuries. However, they face severe degradation due to age, environmental factors, and lack of proper digital preservation.

Our project addresses this challenge by creating the largest publicly available, multi-script dataset of palm leaf manuscripts, featuring scripts like Sundanese, Balinese, and Khmer. We develop state-of-the-art computer vision and deep learning methodologies to tackle key challenges including image enhancement, layout analysis, character classification, and text recognition.

Project Tasks & Details

Our research is structured around four core tasks, each addressing a specific challenge in the digital analysis of palm leaf manuscripts:

Data Collection

Acquisition and annotation of diverse palm leaf manuscripts across Southeast Asian regions and scripts.

Document Enhancement

Advanced image processing and restoration techniques to improve degraded manuscript quality.

Isolated Glyph Classification

Deep learning models for accurate classification of individual characters across different scripts.

Text Recognition

End-to-end text recognition systems for complete manuscript transcription and digital preservation.

Manuscript Collections

Sundanese Manuscripts

Originating from West Java, these manuscripts are written in the Old Sundanese script. The collection showcases the script's distinctive features including complex vowel markers and unique character combinations.

Balinese Manuscripts

From Bali and Lombok, these texts cover a rich array of topics. The intricate Balinese script features a mix of base consonants and sophisticated diacritical marks.

Khmer Manuscripts

From Cambodia, these use one of the oldest scripts in Southeast Asia. The Khmer script is known for its curvilinear shapes and unique structural properties.

Mixed Script Dataset

This collection combines samples from all three scripts to enable robust multi-script analysis, script identification, and cross-linguistic document understanding.

Conclusion & Impact

Research Contribution

This research introduces novel methodologies (e.g., PALM-GANs for enhancement, EFF for classification, and SADA for text synthesis) that establish new standards in historical document analysis.

Cultural Impact

Beyond technical advancements, this work plays a crucial role in the digital preservation of Southeast Asian cultural heritage. By making contents of these manuscripts more accessible, we support education and cultural conservation.

Future Work

Future directions include expanding the dataset to more scripts (e.g., Javanese, Lontara), integrating multimodal approaches, and developing real-time recognition systems.
